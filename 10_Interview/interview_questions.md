# Interview Questions for Prof. R. Venkatesh Babu and VAL Students

## Questions for Prof. R. Venkatesh Babu
### Vision and Lab Direction
1. The Vision and AI Lab has recently published extensively on 3D/4D Gaussian representations and generative flow models. Where do you see the most critical unresolved bottlenecks in these areas over the next 3-5 years?
2. With your recent CVPR 2026 paper, "Rethinking Dataset Distillation: Hard Truths about Soft Labels," how do you view the trade-off between dataset compression and the preservation of long-tail or rare features in real-world applications?
3. What qualities or technical skills do you look for most in prospective Ph.D. students joining your lab right now?

### Deep Technical
4. In the context of "AdaptiveSplat," how do you envision texture-aware controllable 3D Gaussian allocation evolving to handle highly reflective or transparent surfaces in feed-forward reconstruction?
5. Your recent work on mitigating diversity collapse in flow models via feature self-guidance is fascinating. Do you believe this self-guidance approach can be generalized across other generative paradigms like discrete diffusion or auto-regressive models?

## Questions for Ph.D. Students
### Lab Culture & Mentorship
1. How would you describe the day-to-day research culture at VAL? How does Prof. Venkatesh Babu balance giving students independence versus providing hands-on technical guidance?
2. How collaborative are the projects within the lab? Are students encouraged to co-author and cross-pollinate ideas across different sub-fields (e.g., mixing federated learning with 3D vision)?
3. What compute resources and engineering support are available to students for resource-intensive tasks like training large diffusion or 4D Gaussian models?

### Technical / Paper-Specific
4. Regarding "Towards Alias-Free 4D Gaussian Representations," what were the most significant engineering hurdles you faced when implementing motion-aware filtering? 
5. For the 2025 paper "Objects in Generated Videos Are Slower Than They Appear," what physical priors do you think are still fundamentally missing from current video generation architectures?
